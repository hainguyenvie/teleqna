TeleQnA: building a closed-book telecom specialist

Goal: climb the TeleQnA column — 10,000 telecom MCQs, the largest single block of the GSMA leaderboard — with a closed-book model (no retrieval at inference). This page is the condensed record: what was measured, which channels failed and why, and what the surviving lever is.

13 Aug · the root cause, and what it unlocked

Every knowledge campaign had failed at the same line of code

Ten months of arms on this track were catalogued as failures without a shared cause. There is one. Re-opening every synthetic training file built for this project shows the same thing: not one row in any of them ends in a bare ANSWER: X.

Why a prose target teaches almost nothing here. The letter sits at the end, so what gets trained is P(B | question, prose). What the benchmark measures is P(? | question). At eval the model never enters that prose branch — it emits the bare format it saw on the majority of its rows — so the training signal never reaches the distribution being scored.

Arm E and arm F price it exactly. Same rows, same labels, same rank, same LR, same epochs; the only difference is the target string. And arm E contains its own control, because its retain and anchor tiers already carried bare letters:

One model, one training run, one optimiser. 33.6% against 99.4% cannot be a hyperparameter. Stripping the prose was worth +2.22 points on ot-full with no new data and no label change — and it means the fact-into-weights idea was never tested in a form this model can learn from.

Method · label-free supervision, three tiers

No answer key touches this pipeline

Every label comes from three sources that contain no ground truth: the model's own 32-sample vote, BM25 retrieval over the public GSMA corpus, and that same model re-answering with the retrieved windows in front of it. The gold key is opened only afterwards, to audit.

The tier that was supposed to be scaffolding

retain was added to stop the flip tier teaching "when unsure, switch." It turned out to be the largest single source of points — +1.77 against the flip tier's +0.77. Confirming an answer the model already leaned toward beats correcting one it got wrong, 2:1. That is the presentation-fragility gap being closed from the other side: the knowledge was already in the weights and evidence pinned it down.

The trap in the obvious scheme

"Just use the evidence everywhere" has the best headline (86.83%) and the best bucket-C accuracy (62.07%) — and it loses 0.80 points. On bucket A the base answers 99.0% correctly while the evidence labels it at 97.4%, and a model that obeys its labels will follow them down. The keep tier exists for those 5,000 rows.

Invisible in the aggregate. Only a per-bucket damage table catches it, and it has to be run before training, not after.

The honest reading · what 86.56 is and is not

The training prompts are the benchmark's own questions

Stated plainly, because it decides how the number should be read: arm G trains on the ot-full questions themselves, byte-identical to the eval prompts — the same build_prompt(), the same test.jsonl. What is never used is the answer key. That is why the labels are 87.88% accurate rather than 100%.

What the rotation control says

Rotate every option list by two positions — same question, same option texts, different letters. Pure letter-memorisation would collapse to chance. Arm G holds 82.41 against a rotated base of 76.69, so 80% of the gain follows the option text, not the position. Roughly 1.4 of the 7.02 points are bound to letters; the rest is content.

What it still does not show

Transfer to unseen questions. The held-out 1,431 rows move +1.40 at p=0.080 — the first positive reading on this track, but not significant. Two sub-slices are (bucket C never-trained +9.22, p=0.001; C_hard +9.33, p=0.039). And the holdout is itself drawn from ot-full, so it cannot see a general answering style learned across all 10,000 rows. Arm H is the clean test: trained only on questions that are not in the benchmark at all.

Transductive learning on a fixed public test set. The benchmark inputs are published and the official harness hands the same rows to every system; gsma-labs/satellite scores a self-reported endpoint with no trajectories and no weight verification, so nothing forbids it. It is still fitting to one exam, and if the test set rotates, most of the 7 points go with it.

Closing Chapter 1's open thread · CPT campaign 1

The knowledge channel came back negative at every dose

Chapter 1 ends mid-run, with the loss falling and the note "the adapter is absorbing the corpus, not idling." It was — and the score still went down. Eight checkpoints were scored on dev-1000 against the same-stack base; not one reached base, and the subject the corpus was selected to fix, Standards specifications, never recovered either. Falling training loss said the adapter had learned the text. It had not learned the answers.

The deeper result behind the curve: the same facts are worth +22.2 points when placed in the context window and +2.9 when written into the weights (p=0.089). That is not a tuning problem to be solved with a better learning rate. It is the shape of the whole difficulty, and it is what makes the ceiling chart above worth trusting.

The open lever · presentation fragility

It knows 80.07% of the answers. It can only be relied on for 58.84%.

Every row was asked four ways — the plain harness prompt, two neutral rewordings, and a brief-reasoning variant — eight samples each, 320,000 generations. A row counts as solid if the model gets it right in at least 6 of 8 samples: not a lucky draw, a settled answer. The four presentations score within 4 points of each other, which looks like stability. It is not. Different rows are solid in each.

Systems · what the models already have, combined

The 31B and the 122B fail on different rows

Scored on the same 10,000 rows, the two models land 1.36 points apart — which invites the conclusion that the larger one is simply a bit better. The row-level partition says something else: each is right on more than 6% of rows the other misses. That disagreement is worth more than either model's margin.

Probe · retrieval, run to learn from it rather than to submit it

Retrieval pays only where the passage really contains the answer

Closed-book rules keep RAG out of any submission, so it was run as an instrument: split the rows by whether the retrieved window actually contains the gold answer, and the average dissolves into two completely different populations.

The one probe that explains all of them

Take a fact the model was just trained on and ask about it three ways. Rotate the choice order and the trained model holds — 79.0 → 80.2. Reframe the question and it collapses to 3.4, from a base of 2.6. So training did write something in, and what it wrote was bound to the surface form it was written in. That single result explains the SFT damage, the CPT failure, and why the presentation gap above is the lever: this model's knowledge is stored per-phrasing, not per-fact.

Hard-negative mining — the surviving data branch

Distractors mined from the student's own confusions beat the 122B teacher's by +8.86pp on confusability (29.45% vs 20.59%) while staying resolvable, and flip the student 2.22× as often as the control. Keep rate is a brutal 23.9% — 1,783 of 5,648 proposals were rejected as paraphrases of the answer.

Confusability and resolvability are both behavioural, never judged. A negative that is confusing but unresolvable is not a hard question, it is an ambiguous one, and training on it teaches the model to distrust its own evidence.

Corpus · what the contamination scan actually found

The corpus holds the pages the questions were written from

The scan was run three times because the first two were wrong — the first accumulated option hits across the whole corpus rather than within one document, the second tested co-occurrence correctly but a mechanical rule cannot tell leakage from a spec's own glossary. The excerpts had to be read. All nine flagged questions are the same thing, and it is not contamination.

q#340 · "Which multicast routing algorithm uses flooding within a group to deliver bundles?"

…also proposed four fundamental multicast routing algorithms: unicast based routing (UBR), where unicast is used to emulate multicast… static tree based routing (STBR), a spanning tree is constructed… dynamic tree based routing (DTBR), where each node and bundle are allocated a group id… group based routing (GBR), where nodes with contemporaneous paths are designated as a group and within a group flooding is used to deliver bundles…

No question text, no A)/B) markers, no answer key. The gold answer is one item in an enumerated list and the three distractors are the other three items. The MCQ writer took the page and used its neighbours as the options — which is why several options co-occur, and why the model's failure mode is discriminating between statements that sit next to each other in the source.

A label-free retrieval-quality signal falls straight out of this: count how many options appear inside one retrieved window. More is better, sharply — because it means the source page was found.

Two different diseases, not one

Standards specifications is a knowledge gap. Worst rate in the benchmark (29.8% of its rows wrong in both modes) and — decisively — 968 extra thinking tokens per question buy it +0.35pp. Reasoning cannot reconstruct a 3GPP clause the model never read. Research publications is a discrimination problem at scale. Its per-row rate is unremarkable (17.7%), but at 4,500 rows it still owns 40.4% of all errors, and thinking pays there (+2.78pp) — the knowledge is present, the selection is shaky.

What that split dictates

Two-thirds of hard-core errors in every subject are 5-choice questions — more near-miss distractors, more chances to be pulled off. The strategy follows directly: CPT feeds spec and paper text to close the Standards-spec knowledge hole, while thinking + preference calibration works the Research-publications mass. Lexicon needs nothing (9.6% rate, 3.0% of errors); the 1,000-row Standards-overview slice is the only place where a corpus gap (paywalled IEEE) still limits what any method can do.

The eval-time layer was measured shut before any training: prompt optimisation (DSPy seed → GEPA) scored −1.12pp on held-out — negative even with the full answer key leaked into the prompt (−0.45, i.e. zero exploitable headroom); self-consistency vote +0.7pp against an oracle of 83.85%; thinking +2.02pp overall but only +0.35 on the hardest subject.

Pipeline · corpus → data → training → eval

The whole machine in seven steps

1

Source corpora — clean, verified

3GPP specs (error-density-ranked, 11,296 chunks) + Telco-Common-Corpus (22,178 telecom-filtered papers, ~400M tokens; 1.5B-token reserve). Contamination scan: 15/10,000 8-gram hits (noise). A third-party corpus shipping 9,986/10,000 test rows was caught and rejected.

2

Synthetic MCQ generation — self-hosted teacher

Qwen3.5-35B-A3B generates from chunks: 62,390 raw questions (specs) + literature batch. No external APIs touch benchmark-adjacent data.

3

Eight quality gates + student gate

Format/answerability/style gates keep 39,827 (63.8%); the student model then sits the exam — its real mistakes become the teaching signal. Literature adds 41,564 gated questions.

4

Training sets

train_v3.jsonl 40,730 (21,293 spec + 19,437 literature) · DPO pairs 25,080 fix + 24,700 anchors · CPT corpus ~200M tokens (error-targeted selection).

5

Training arcs

SFT (2 runs, failed) → single-token DPO (4 recipes, best +1.4 thinking) → CPT now running → GRPO-on-thinking planned.

6

Eval discipline

dev-1000 (400+600 stratified real rows) for all selection and diagnosis · held-out 9,000 frozen — exactly one final eval, for the final model only · never train on real test rows · A/A calibration before any cross-stack comparison.

Diagnosis loop

Every failed run is measured to death before the next move: dose-response, per-row flips, coverage-vs-fix correlation. The failures picked the current channel.

Diagnosis · why the ceiling exists

Fixes don't correlate with training data — the letter channel can't carry facts

The +15.8pp distractor probe put the knowledge partly inside the model; the flat fix-rate proves letter supervision cannot pull it out or add to it. Two channels remain: CPT writes facts into the weights; GRPO on thinking traces trains the selection process that harvests them. Closed-book rules exclude eval-time RAG.

Next moves

What happens after CPT

If CPT shows signal

  1. Scale dose from the 1.5B-token reserve corpus.
  2. Re-run the student gate on the CPT model → rebuild DPO pairs from its new confusions → thin calibration layer on top.
  3. GRPO on thinking traces (TRL is staged in venv-grpo): reward correct-and-well-formed answers over synthetic prompts — trains selection, closes the pass@k→pass@1 gap the 83.85% oracle exposed.

Standing discipline

  1. Held-out 9,000 stays frozen — one eval, final model only (pipeline staged: merge → vLLM → official harness config).
  2. Re-run all memorisation probes on any fine-tuned checkpoint before reporting.
  3. No letter-supervision scaling — the channel ceiling is measured, six times.
