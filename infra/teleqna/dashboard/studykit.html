Part ATeleQnAfinal · 22/09/2026

01Task and constraint

TeleQnA is the largest column of the GSMA Open Telco leaderboard: 10,000 of the 19,588 questions the average is computed over. The submitted artefact must be one Qwen3-8B answering closed-book.

Test questions may be used to select public source documents through retrieval. The answer key and the explanation field are measurement instruments only — they never enter a generation prompt, a data filter, or a training set. The benchmark's 10,000 rows are never trained on.

Baseline is 71.85. With eight retrieved windows in context the same model scores 83.75. The entire track is the question of how much of that +11.9 can be moved into the weights. It ended at +10.4.

04What the diagnosis found

Never is the harder population and it is not a knowledge gap: 413 of 428 inspected cases had the gold string verbatim in a window and repeated at least five times in the kit. Reading samples showed one pattern — the model picks a sibling entity from the same passage: EDGE-1 → EDGE-3, state N8 → N9, first-order → second-order Markov, 10 cm → 1.5 m. This is an attribute-to-entity binding failure.

Sibling-contrast was built from that reading: exam items whose distractors are other siblings from the same window, with every option and the evidence required verbatim in that window. It is the largest single-stage gain since vote distillation (+0.62, +0.79 on the rotated control).

Sibling-contrast item · window w048526 (IEEE C95.1 effects table)
Q: What is an effect of RF/MW radiation exposure on children?
   A) Decreased memory, attention, and slower reaction time in school children  ← correct
   B) Retarded learning in rats indicating a deficit in spatial "working memory"
   C) Increased blood pressure in healthy men
   D) Damage to eye cells when combined with commonly used glaucoma medications
   E) Changes in sleep patterns and REM type sleep

evidence: "20) - Decreased memory, attention, and slower reaction time in school children"

All four distractors are true statements taken verbatim from the same table. They are wrong only because they answer a different question, so topic recognition cannot solve the item.

Extraction, not storage, was the bottleneck at vd6. On 1,214 questions the checkpoint had not fixed, base plus the retrieved windows answers 83% correctly — the fact is recoverable from the source, so the failure lay in retrieving it from the weights.

Three packing bugs were worth about 4 points combined: self-replay anchors that taught the base's own wrong beliefs (+1.50 once fixed), a chat/raw-text mode mismatch (2.7 points recoverable), and a missing <|im_end|> that let the model write past the answer letter — the same checkpoint scored 39.23 under the strict parser and 77.81 on the first letter.

06Measurement discipline

  • Every arm reports recover, broke, the rotated-choice control, and strict against first-letter parsing. A/A noise floor is 0.50 points.
  • Three scorers agree. Internal first-letter 82.27, the harness replica run_baseline.py 82.22, and the official gsma-labs/evals runner 0.822–0.823 on the same checkpoint. The image was also pulled back from Docker Hub byte-identical and re-scored from its own entrypoint.
  • Coverage is measured functionally, not by string overlap — the string measure gave a meaningless 20.5% because TeleQnA options are GPT-3.5 rephrasings rather than source quotes.
  • Every context claim carries a rotated-context control; the noise floor for "some passage is present" is about 9%.
  • Contamination: 2,937 test questions appear verbatim inside the kit, because the source windows contain the same sentences GPT-3.5 used to write them. The gain is equal on both groups (+7.4 with overlap, +7.1 without) and no copy of the dataset was found in the corpus.
  • Label noise is estimated at 3–5% by a 31B judge reading source windows, putting the ceiling with perfect knowledge at roughly 95–97.

07Where it ended

The target of 85 was not reached; the track stopped at 82.3, 2.7 points short. The remaining ~1,780 errors were decomposed by letting OTel-31B read each question's retrieved windows:

Groupshare of errorsWhat would move it
Retrieved windows do not decide the question52%new sources, or a base that already knows it (31B closed-book gets 38% of these)

What stopped TeleQnA is knowledge, not decoding. On the final model the wrong answers have a letter margin of 0.85; 55% are confidently wrong and only 11% are near-ties, so a perfect tie-breaker could add at most +1.3. Prompting, voting and regex post-processing all measured at zero or below.

Part BORANBenchin progress · 22/09/2026

08Task and constraint

ORANBench is 1,500 four-option questions on the O-RAN ALLIANCE specifications, split evenly into easy, medium and hard. The rules are TeleQnA's without change: one Qwen3-8B, closed-book, no training on benchmark rows, questions usable only to select public source documents.

The leaderboard's average is an unweighted mean of seven columns, so these 1,500 questions carry the same weight as TeleQnA's 10,000 — at a sixth of the evaluation cost. The column is also the one where an 8B has the most room: 0.6733 for qwen3-8b on the board, 0.9407 for AT&T's 8.3B model, 0.9000 for the best closed model.

Two properties of the data change the plan. First, all 1,500 test questions sit inside the public ORAN-Bench-13K; training on that set is training on the test, which is the simplest explanation for a 0.94. We do not. Second, the answer key is badly skewed: A or B is correct 65.3% of the time and the gold option is the longest 48.3% of the time, so an always-longest heuristic already scores 43.2% without reading the question. With n = 1,500 one standard error is about 1 point, against 0.4 on TeleQnA.

10Ceilings, measured before training

Two readings of Fig 3 shaped the run. The TeleQnA model, with no ORAN data at all, already scores 76.53 — telecom knowledge transfers, and it barely moves under rotation (75.87). And wrong O-RAN text in the prompt pushes the base below closed-book (63.27), while the Telco model ignores it (76.67): the base is easily misled by plausible spec text, so the kit's distractors must come from the same window, as in sibling-contrast.

The rotation gap is the headline. ORANBench's key is skewed toward A and B and toward the longest option, and the synthetic MCQs in the kit carry a length bias of their own. The trained model leans on position more than the base did: NOT/EXCEPT loses 8.8 points under rotation. On TeleQnA the same error was worked down by vote and ensemble distillation — the big run lost 2.1 under rotation, the final model loses 0.8.

One assumption from the task description is refuted. 273 questions refer to "the context" or "the document" that is never provided, and were expected to be guessable at best. ob1b scores 83.9% on them against 83.8% on the rest: they are answerable from knowledge, and they are not a ceiling.

ORANBench is already past TeleQnA's final score after one knowledge run, because its questions are closer to its corpus (context ceiling 90.3 against 83.8). What separates 83.8 from the 90 the context arms reach is mostly the same two errors TeleQnA spent its second half on: consistency across orderings, and extraction.
